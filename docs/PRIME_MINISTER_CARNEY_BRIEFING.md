## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & THE CANADIAN CAPITAL PARADOX

Canada does not suffer from a shortage of investable capital. Domestic pension funds manage over **$3.2 Trillion CAD** in assets; global infrastructure funds, sovereign wealth funds, and private credit institutions hold historic dry powder targeting the energy transition, critical minerals, and AI compute.

The bottleneck is **structural, informational, and jurisdictional**:
1. **Opaque Multi-Jurisdictional Permitting**: Capital allocators cannot observe real-time progression across federal (IAAC, CER), provincial, and municipal gazettes.
2. **Disconnected Capital Stacking**: Tax incentives (Clean Tech ITC, Clean Electricity ITC, CMITC) operate in silos from concessionary financing (Canada Infrastructure Bank, Canada Growth Fund), leading to sub-optimal stacking and capital paralysis.
3. **Unanticipated Supply Chain Squeezes**: Downstream procurement requirements (EPC contracts, high-voltage transformers, specialized metallurgical processing) are surfaced only after Final Investment Decision (FID), causing multi-year inflation and delay.

**The Solution:** **CanadaOpportunityGraph (COG)** and the **Canada Economic Graph Schema (CEGS 0.1)**.

COG is the country's first open-standard, evidence-grounded national economic graph. It replaces subjective claims with **cryptographic SHA-256 provenance**, evaluates projects through **deterministic 0–100 scoring engines**, and maps downstream supplier requirements before shovels hit the ground.

* **Flagship Assets**: Darlington New Nuclear SMR Project ($7.7B CAD, 4x BWRX-300 units), Oneida Energy Storage ($500M CAD, 250MW/1,000MWh).

* **Flagship Assets**: Crawford Nickel-Cobalt Sulphide Project ($3.5B CAD, Timmins, ON — 2nd largest nickel reserve globally with net-zero carbon capture), Baie-James Lithium Corridor ($1.2B CAD, QC).

### Pillar 4: Indigenous Economic Reconciliation Through Equity Co-Ownership

### Pillar 5: Arctic Sovereignty & Northern Resilience
